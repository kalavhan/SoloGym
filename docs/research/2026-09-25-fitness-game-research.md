**SoloGym research brief — 25 September 2026**

The concept is feasible in principle: a fitness companion can share a character and rewards with a mobile action RPG, a procedural tower, and a social gym. Technical feasibility does not establish that the game will be enjoyable, that its exercise recommendations are clinically validated, or that it will improve fitness. Those outcomes require later evaluation.

This document records research and proposed product decisions. It is not an approved feature specification, exercise prescription, development plan, or legal determination. No engine, architecture, implementation schedule, or final screen inventory has been selected. The agreed sequence remains research, follow-up decisions, a high-level plan, visual renders, style approval, then further planning and development.

The confirmed direction is Android and iOS for ages 15+, targeting the US, Canada, and Latin America; bodyweight and equipment workouts; automatically assembled routines or editable presets; English and Spanish with automatic language selection and a manual override; account creation and Google login; and a dark manhua-inspired System interface with blue/violet windows, illustrated characters, and an isometric world. The original scope also includes male/female characters, gear, workout bosses, difficulty choices, rankings, friends, limited chat, a Boxer class, an endless tower, and optional fasting tracking. Disability-specific exercise programming is a later expansion; general interface accessibility still belongs in the initial design.

The GitHub API confirmed that [kalavhan/SoloGym](https://github.com/kalavhan/SoloGym) is public and has no commits. The local workspace also had no commits and no configured remote when inspected. Nothing has been pushed.

**Fitness programming needs separate adult and teen guidance.** WHO recommends adults accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, or an equivalent combination each week, plus muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days. For ages 5–17, the recommendation is an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily across the week, with vigorous activity and muscle/bone strengthening on at least three days. These are population guidelines, not a required starting dose for every user. Everyday movement, sports, and play contribute; a teen does not need a daily hour-long lifting session. Older adults also benefit from balance-oriented multicomponent activity. [WHO guidelines, 2020](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128)

Youth resistance training can be beneficial when it is developmentally appropriate and emphasizes technique and supervision. Experience and competence matter alongside age. AAP recommends evaluating illness, injury, or overuse before exercise resumes. A phone app cannot verify safe technique merely because a user completes a set. [AAP resistance-training guidance, 2020](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/Guidance-on-Resistance-Training-for-Children.aspx)

ACSM's 2026 adult resistance-training guidance supports home routines, bodyweight exercises, and bands as well as conventional gym equipment. Strength, muscle growth, and power emphasize different training variables. Training to failure and complicated periodization are not consistently necessary for the average healthy adult. These adult findings should not be copied directly into teen prescriptions. [ACSM position-stand summary, March 2026](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)

The following is a proposed translation of that evidence into SoloGym's training choices. These are product categories, not validated automatic prescriptions.

| Training focus | Role in SoloGym | Important distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General fitness | A balanced entry path combining manageable strength and aerobic activity | Starting ability and sustainable participation matter more than selecting an advanced routine |
| Strength | Gradual improvement in the resistance or exercise variation a person can handle | Raw kilograms should not determine public rank or battle power |
| Muscle growth | A structured resistance path with appropriate workload and recovery | Visible muscularity is not a reliable measure of strength or health |
| Muscular endurance | Repeated submaximal work suited to the person's ability | Unlimited repetitions should not become an XP farming mechanism |
| Aerobic endurance | Walking, cycling, other suitable activities, and optional intervals | Intervals describe a work/rest format; “Hard” need not mean all-out HIIT |
| Mobility, balance, and recovery | Supporting movement options and recovery-friendly participation | These are not substitutes for every strength or aerobic goal |

**Automatic workouts should be assembled from reviewed content.** Recommend structured presets and an exercise library reviewed by appropriately qualified exercise professionals, with youth-specific review. Choose a routine using age group, experience, goals, available equipment, time, recent training, and reported readiness. Allow edits and substitutions while preserving the purpose and a manageable dose. This is a proposed approach to control quality; the cited exercise evidence does not validate an AI workout generator.

Random variation belongs primarily in the fantasy encounters. Physical routines need enough continuity to learn technique and assess progress. Progression can change exercise variation, repetitions, sets, resistance, or duration as appropriate; it should not blindly increase all of them every time a boss is defeated. Selecting a muscular avatar or a high game difficulty should not automatically prescribe heavier exercise.

| Session choice | Proposed meaning | Reward treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | A reduced session with easier variations, less work, or more recovery | Full recognition for following an appropriate adjusted plan |
| Medium — default | The user's normal, manageable session | Baseline progression |
| Hard | An optional bounded increase for a ready, suitably experienced user | No automatic competitive multiplier for choosing harder exercise |
| Rest or stop | A legitimate option for illness, pain, injury, or planned recovery | Preserve earned progress; avoid punishment for stopping |

For aerobic work, CDC describes moderate relative effort as roughly 5–6 out of 10, and vigorous effort as 7–8. The talk test provides a simpler cue. These aerobic descriptions should not be confused with resistance-training RPE or repetitions-in-reserve scales. [CDC activity intensity, December 2025](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)

The original consecutive-exercise-days ranking needs adjustment. AAP youth sports guidance recommends rest from organized training and gradual increases to reduce overtraining and burnout. This does not prohibit ordinary daily movement. I recommend a consistency streak that recognizes the agreed schedule, protects planned recovery days, and supports illness pauses. This changes the incentive from doing progressively more exercise to sustaining an appropriate routine. [AAP overtraining guidance, July 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/sports-injuries/Pages/Too-Much-Too-Soon-Overtraining.aspx)

**Height and weight cannot reconstruct a person's physique.** BMI does not distinguish fat, muscle, and bone, or show fat distribution. The proposed 90 kg/160 cm example therefore cannot determine muscularity from those numbers alone. CDC also uses different BMI interpretation for ages 2–19 and adults 20+, a different boundary from adult exercise guidance. I recommend keeping BMI classifications out of character creation and avoiding body-fat estimates derived only from height and weight. [CDC BMI guidance, December 2025](https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/about/index.html)

The likely intended term is **mesomorph**, alongside endomorph and ectomorph. The Heath–Carter method describes present physique using continuous components of relative fatness, musculoskeletal robustness, and linearity. It does not establish three fixed game classes. Its original manual was available in indexed excerpts, although the full PDF did not open during this research. [Carter's somatotype manual](https://mdthinducollege.org/ebooks/statistics/Heath-CarterManual.pdf)

I found no basis in the reviewed prescribing guidelines for making a self-selected somatotype the main driver of exercise recommendations. Recommend omitting it from required onboarding. Users can instead adjust body size/proportions and muscularity independently, with male/female character options and freedom to use a fantasy appearance. If measurements suggest a starting silhouette, identify it as an editable artistic approximation. Use neutral labels such as “build” and “muscle definition”; do not assign power bonuses to thinness, body size, or an idealized appearance. AAP describes how weight-related teasing can harm well-being and discourage physical activity. The proposed character controls are a design response to that evidence. [AAP weight-stigma guidance](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/obesity/Pages/Teasing-and-Bullying.aspx)

For personal data, distinguish the account's private fitness information from the character's public identity. Proposed private information includes age eligibility, height/weight when useful, training experience, goals, equipment, and readiness. Public information can be limited to an alias, chosen avatar, gear, and permitted game achievements. Character presentation should not silently determine medical assumptions or disclosure of personal information.

**Gamification is promising, with limits to the evidence.** In the STEP UP randomized trial, 602 adults used wearable-tracked walking goals. Gamification with support, collaboration, or competition increased activity over 24 weeks; the competition group averaged an adjusted 920 additional daily steps compared with control. The population and intervention differ substantially from teens doing strength routines or playing an action RPG. [STEP UP trial, JAMA Internal Medicine, 2019](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2749761)

In BE ACTIVE, 1,062 adults at elevated cardiovascular risk received gamification, financial incentives, both, or attention control. Gamification added about 538 daily steps relative to control during the 12-month intervention. Sustained benefit after the intervention was clearest for the combined treatment. These findings support exploring meaningful goals and feedback; they do not establish that more time inside a tower game improves fitness. [BE ACTIVE randomized trial, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38583084/)

My proposed connection is: complete and log an appropriate workout; logged sets advance that session's boss; the session yields coins, gear, and fitness progression; the player uses the character in a separately played tower. This avoids needing to handle combat controls during a lift. Rest days should still permit tower play.

Game progression should recognize plan completion and personal improvement rather than kilograms lifted, calories burned, fasting duration, or body shape. Bodyweight users and beginners need a viable path. Manually logged data cannot prove a workout happened, so early rankings should be described as recreational, with no cash prizes or health claims based on them. Distinguish earned fitness progression from tower progression so unlimited combat does not erase the meaning of fitness achievements. These are proposed game-economy choices, not medical findings.

**Fasting requires a narrower feature than the original buff system.** NIDDK advises teens against trying to lose weight through fasting, meal skipping, or severe restriction because growing bodies need adequate energy and nutrients. Recommend no fasting timer, fasting quests, or fasting bonuses for under-18 accounts. [NIDDK guide for teenagers](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/take-charge-health-guide-teenagers)

Johns Hopkins advises discussing intermittent fasting with a clinician and identifies people who should avoid it, including under-18s, pregnant/breastfeeding people, people with type 1 diabetes taking insulin, and people with an eating-disorder history. This is not an exhaustive suitability screen. A warning only about unusually long fasts would miss relevant concerns. [Johns Hopkins fasting guidance, updated April 2026](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/intermittent-fasting-what-is-it-and-how-does-it-work)

A 2020 randomized trial studied four-hour and six-hour eating windows over eight weeks in adults with obesity. That establishes that 20:4 has been studied; it does not establish 20 hours as universally safe or appropriate for every adult, and it says nothing about rewarding teens for fasting. [Cienfuegos et al., original trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32673591/)

If retained, the adult feature should be an optional neutral log, off by default, without XP, combat buffs, streaks, or ranking based on fasting. The requested 20-hour maximum can be a limit on supported plans, not a safety certification or target to work toward. Ending early should not lose progress. Avoid claims that a timer proves autophagy, fat burning, or a particular health benefit. Recovery-themed rewards can remain available through nonrestrictive activities for all users. These proposed changes were accepted by the user before design.

**The social gym is feasible, but censorship alone is insufficient.** Google Play requires ongoing moderation suited to user-generated content, including reporting for public content and blocking for direct interactions. Preset phrases do not eliminate abuse through usernames, repeated messages, or behavior. [Google Play user-generated-content policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en)

Recommend an initial gym with movement, emotes, localized preset phrases, friend requests, and friends-only preset private messages. Each recipient can see a selected phrase in their own language. Include mute/block/report and controls on invitations and spam. Free text should follow a decision about ongoing moderation and child protection, rather than simply adding a profanity list. For teens, recommend private profiles, limited discoverability, and no unsolicited stranger private messages. Public state/country/world boards for teens remain a specific unresolved choice; a friends-only default is preferable.

**The confirmed minimum age is 15+, targeting the US, Canada, and Latin America.** Exact country availability and applicable consent requirements remain release decisions. The regional research below is context; age 15 is not a worldwide independent-consent rule.

Google's health policy explicitly covers games using activity data to advance gameplay. Health-related information still needs appropriate privacy disclosures and handling. For regional rankings, voluntarily selected broad regions can avoid continuous GPS collection; they are not verified residence data. [Google Play health policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511?hl=en)

**Accounts, purchases, and language are feasible with platform constraints.** Account creation and Google sign-in can represent the same player account. Google provides official Android sign-in support. [Google sign-in documentation](https://developer.android.com/identity/sign-in/credential-manager-siwg)

For an iOS App Store release, Apple's login rule generally requires an equivalent privacy-preserving alternative when Google authenticates the primary account, subject to its exceptions. Sign in with Apple is the straightforward candidate; ordinary email signup should not be assumed sufficient. Apps offering account creation also need in-app account deletion. Apple's digital-purchase and randomized-paid-reward rules affect gear sales. [Apple review guidelines, sections 4.8, 5.1.1, and 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

Recommend purchasable cosmetics and themes, with combat power earned through play. “Basic,” “Rare,” and “Ultra Rare” can describe earned rarity; “Premium” should describe availability or styling rather than superior power. Avoid paid randomized loot in the first version. Google Play's billing requirements apply to digital goods subject to specified exceptions and regional programs. [Google Play payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)

English/Spanish auto-selection with a persistent manual override is practical. Offer “System default,” “English,” and “Español.” Android supports per-app language settings. Renders should demonstrate Spanish text fit as well as English; names, safety copy, dates, numbers, and measurement units need attention too. [Android language documentation](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/app-languages)

**The action game remains an engineering and art assessment.** An isometric camera, free movement, a Boxer with three attacks and a special, connected rooms, and increasingly difficult enemies are conventional game capabilities. A procedural tower can generate floors as needed rather than storing infinitely many levels. It still needs deliberately designed rooms, encounter variety, and difficulty limits; endlessly increasing enemy health alone is unlikely to provide lasting variety.

The largest scope drivers are the quality and breadth of character/gear art, fitting gear to different silhouettes, responsive combat, live social presence, moderation, and reviewed exercise content. These cannot be estimated credibly before the product boundaries and approved visual target are clearer. A persistent shared world and an instanced social gym are different scopes; the first brief does not yet require a seamless MMO. Additional mini-games are underspecified and should not be invented as committed launch features.

The proposed visual interpretation is dark stone and cosmic environments, luminous blue/violet System windows, manhua character art, prominent character/equipment views, and readable combat controls. It should have an original identity. Portrait menus/workout screens plus landscape tower combat is a candidate for your decision. Interface readability, touch targets, reduced motion, and bilingual text fit still matter within that style. After clarification and the high-level plan, the renders can establish the exact appearance before implementation choices are made.

The follow-up decisions are now recorded below. Detailed implementation planning and development remain behind the requested visual approval gate.


After the research, the user confirmed ages 15+, Android/iOS, and the US, Canada, and Latin America as target regions. They accepted adult-only neutral fasting without buffs, recovery-protecting streaks, preset public/private chat, private teen profiles, and cosmetic purchases without purchasable fitness XP or combat advantages.

The user clarified that automation means generating the exercise plan for a boss, **not automatic exercise detection or repetition counting**. Manual sets, repetitions, load, and timers are the working logging model. Camera capture, pose tracking, and wearable detection are outside this version. Workout bosses and the separately played action tower remain the proposed interpretation.

For Canada, meaningful consent must be understandable and appropriate to maturity; health information generally requires express consent. A Google login does not supply fitness-data consent. [Canadian privacy commissioner guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/appropriate-handling-of-personal-information/collecting-personal-information-and-consent/consent/gl_omc_201805/) Quebec's private-sector privacy law allows data consent from a minor aged 14+ or their parent/tutor; this does not settle every contract or feature requirement. [Quebec privacy law, section 14](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/p-39.1)

English/Spanish remain the approved design languages. French service/localization obligations require assessment if Quebec is included in distribution, taking account of applicability and software exceptions. [OQLF language guidance](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/francisation/droits_linguistiques/droits/langue-du-commerce-et-des-affaires.html) Latin America is a region rather than one consent jurisdiction. Brazil's ECA Digital took effect in March 2026 and age-assurance guidance is evolving. Portuguese is also a potential localization decision for Brazil. Exact country availability and country-specific age/consent requirements are release-readiness decisions; they need not prevent concept styling. [Brazil ANPD ECA Digital guidance](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/eca-digital/eca-digital-english)
